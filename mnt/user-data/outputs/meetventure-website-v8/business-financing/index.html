Match the structure to the need

The most common expensive mistake is funding the wrong thing with the wrong product - covering a seasonal cash flow gap with a five-year term loan, or buying a $200,000 machine on a line of credit.

  • Working capital gaps - operating line of credit. You draw and repay as cash flow moves.
  • Equipment purchases - term loan or lease, amortized over the asset's useful life.
  • Expansion or leasehold improvements - term loan, sometimes with government program support.
  • Property purchase - that's a commercial mortgage. See commercial lending.

Government-backed options

The Canada Small Business Financing Program lets the federal government share lender risk, which can open doors for businesses that wouldn't clear conventional criteria alone. It's particularly relevant for equipment and leasehold improvements. Program limits and eligibility change over time, so confirm current details before building a plan around it.

What to have ready

  • Two years of financial statements, accountant-prepared where possible
  • 6-12 months of business bank statements
  • A specific use of funds - "$85,000 for a CNC machine that adds capacity" beats "some working capital"
  • Repayment logic tied to real revenue, not optimism
  • Personal financial information for the owners

If your business is young

Under two years of history narrows options rather than eliminating them. Personal credit, available security, and government-backed programs all carry more weight. Being realistic about that upfront saves you from a string of declines that quietly damage your credit file.

Common questions

Questions people actually ask

What types of business financing are available?

Term loans for equipment and expansion, operating lines of credit for working capital, equipment leasing, and government-backed options such as the Canada Small Business Financing Program. The right structure depends on whether you're funding an asset or a cash flow gap.

What is the Canada Small Business Financing Program?

A federal program where the government shares risk with lenders to help small businesses access financing for things like equipment and leasehold improvements. Eligibility and limits are set by the program, so confirm current terms before planning around it.

What do lenders want to see from my business?

Typically two years of financial statements, recent business bank statements, a clear explanation of what the funds are for and how they'll be repaid, and personal financial information from the owners. Newer businesses lean more heavily on projections and personal credit.

Can I get financing for a business less than two years old?

It's harder but not impossible. Options narrow, personal credit and any available security carry more weight, and government-backed programs sometimes help bridge the gap.
